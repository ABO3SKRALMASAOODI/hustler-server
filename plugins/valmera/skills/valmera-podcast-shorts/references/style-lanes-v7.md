# Requested editing styles

Use this contract when the user requests these treatments. It does not impose
them on unrelated briefs. Current user corrections override reference order,
historical taste profiles, and generic editorial defaults. Apply names rather
than ordinal reference numbers. The timing ranges below are user-requested
starting guidance, not measurements of any reference.

## Choose by material, without a default lane

The September 14 correction removes the earlier Style 4 default and automatic
Style 2 priority. A default canvas composition is not a default editing style.
Evaluate every selected idea across the four structures before assigning it:

| Lane | Positive reason to choose it |
| --- | --- |
| `fast-conversation` | The complete spoken story benefits from frequent specific visual evidence or examples while dialogue continues. |
| `hook-to-silent-montage` | A strong complete 5–10-second spoken premise can stop, and available quality assets support a substantial silent visual payoff. |
| `silent-action-to-conversation` | Recognizable relevant action footage makes a compelling opening promise that the conversation fulfills. |
| `headline-conversation` | The exchange, expression, or reasoning carries the idea; clean footage and a persistent headline serve it better than cutaways. |

For each story, record `style_choice_reason` and `closest_alternative` with
the content/asset reason it loses. Do not select by ordinal reference order,
editor availability, ease, or the fact that the source is a conversation.
Make a reasonable targeted asset search before declaring a visual lane unfit.
Record a real asset gap rather than quietly defaulting to Style 4.

The coordinator reviews counts and reasons before dispatch and after each
wave. Dominance like 15–16 Style 4 stories plus one per other lane triggers
reassessment of the actual ideas and asset opportunities. Reassign where the
material warrants it; keep uneven counts when specific evidence supports
them. Counts alone do not justify a lane, equal shares, quotas, or one token
example of each. Do not duplicate stories to manufacture variety.

All lane structure, silence, and headline windows below describe the editorial
portion. Every final retains the separate native Valmera branded ending;
see the branding contract below. Budget its duration within the final limit.

## 1. Fast conversation (`fast-conversation`)

Open on a strong spoken line and deliver one complete spoken story. Use
frequent, fast, relevant B-roll throughout the conversation; keep dialogue
continuous underneath it and return to the speaker deliberately. This is the
proper lane for energetic conversation with rapid cutaways.

Start with roughly 0.3–0.8 seconds for stills/simple cards. Video B-roll may
hold for 2–3 seconds when useful action deserves it, with shorter bursts for
energy; extreme speed is not compulsory. No individual video B-roll insert
may exceed 5 seconds. This cap applies to B-roll, not the main conversation.
A long source file is not a reason to hold a weak shot. Keep essential text readable;
do not cut speech or flash captions to match every visual change.

Do not silently turn this into sparse, slow B-roll or a captions-only short.
If relevant high-quality assets are unavailable, report the specific asset
gap or propose reassignment; do not pad with generic stock. There is no
arbitrary insert quota, but frequent fast B-roll must be visible in the result.

## 2. Hook to silent montage (`hook-to-silent-montage`)

Choose for a premise with a strong visual payoff. Show approximately
5–10 seconds of a complete, powerful spoken premise. Finish the thought,
then end podcast dialogue and its captions. From that transition to the end,
show only a continuous full-frame montage; never return to the podcast scene
or resume dialogue. The montage should occupy most of the short and provide
a relevant visual payoff without inventing facts or associations.

Keep this lane compact. The montage should normally run 10–15 seconds and
must never exceed 15 seconds. The complete editorial program—the spoken hook
plus montage, before the separate native Valmera ending—must never exceed
25 seconds. Aim for a 20–25-second editorial program when the idea genuinely
supports it, but never pad a resolved story or repeat a visual to reach that
range. The native Valmera ending is outside both measurements.

Build the montage around the **person and the actual premise**, not a broad
topic cloud. For each proposed shot, record the visible subject, its verified
identity/provenance, the exact hook or payoff beat it serves, and why a viewer
can understand that connection without knowing an intermediate fact. Prefer
recognizable footage of the person across real stages of their life and work,
the specific places/objects named in the story when verified, and unmistakable
results of that person's work. For a struggle-to-success story, a fast,
intentional before/after progression can contrast authentic earlier material
with identifiable later accomplishments. Do not use generic poverty imagery
as if it depicts the person, or imply that footage from one period depicts
another.

Reject indirect association: generic astronauts are not an Elon Musk shot
merely because they might have ridden a SpaceX vehicle. If the visual link
needs an explanatory sentence or several inferred hops, it is too remote for
this fast montage. A verified SpaceX launch or a recognizable Elon shot may
serve a SpaceX-related payoff; a random rocket or astronaut may not. If enough
direct, rights-usable visual material is unavailable, search specifically for
it, then change lanes or surface the asset gap rather than filling the
montage with adjacent stock. Fast cutting cannot rescue weak relevance.

Starting holds: stills 0.25–0.6 seconds, simple one-word/name cards 0.3–0.8
seconds, and video moments roughly 0.7–3 seconds. A video can comfortably hold
2–3 seconds when its action earns it; no video B-roll insert may exceed
5 seconds. Use bursts and occasional anchor shots, with readable typography
and a deliberate ending. Avoid a uniform slideshow and single-frame flashes.

Every montage shot must be visually new within that short. Do not reuse the
same still, the same source-time window, or a near-identical moment later in
the montage; a crop, zoom, mirror, speed change, color treatment, or text layer
does not make repeated footage unique. Different, clearly distinct moments
from one source video are allowed when each advances the sequence. Reuse in a
different short is allowed, but prefer a fresh equally relevant shot across
the batch. Never sacrifice direct relevance merely to avoid cross-short reuse.

For a no-music brief, the entire montage is intentionally silent: no podcast
voice, clip audio, voiceover, music, or SFX. The user adds a track later. Record
the exact montage start as a music-lift cue; do not claim beat synchronization
without a selected track. A conversation covered with fast B-roll is Style 1,
and automatically fails this lane.

## 3. Silent action to conversation (`silent-action-to-conversation`)

Start with approximately 3–4 seconds of VIDEO of the actual subject visibly
doing something: walking, entering, working, or another recognizable action.
The subject must be identifiable and not visibly speaking. A still picture or
muted talking-head clip does not qualify. Mute the entire opening clip.

Put a readable headline near the top inside the safe picture area. For the
Elon brief it begins exactly `Elon Musk:`; for another subject use the name
specified in that brief. The headline promises the idea that the following
podcast passage explains. Transition into a complete, clean conversation,
remove the opening headline, and use premium restrained dialogue captions.
Record the exact dialogue-start cue for later music placement.

## 4. Persistent-headline conversation (`headline-conversation`)

Choose when the conversation itself is best served by this treatment.
Start directly with a strong spoken idea and develop it through the podcast
footage alone. No B-roll, external cutaways, image montages, action opener,
or title-card interruptions. Natural cuts between actual podcast camera
angles and speakers are allowed. Do not confuse this with `fast-conversation`,
which intentionally uses frequent B-roll.

Use the black-canvas layout from `headline-conversation-references-v7.md`:
a 9:16 file with a wider, straight-edged picture panel inside it, black space
above and below, and a fixed headline immediately above the picture. Keep
the headline visible from the first editorial frame to the last, not just
for the hook. Leave the native Valmera ending unobstructed.
Write one truthful, compelling topic headline, normally one or two lines.
The headline should state the idea or tension, not administrative context.
Do not add a smaller date, interview date, city, venue, or “where this
happened” line beneath it just because metadata is available. If a date or
place is essential to understanding the story, integrate it into the primary
headline or spoken context; otherwise omit it. This also applies to other
lanes with a headline. Avoid source-footage lower thirds or stock overlays
that reintroduce such incidental metadata.
Use a bold/heavy headline font with at least the visible stroke weight of
the dialogue captions, and optional emphasis on one meaningful phrase.
Do not copy another creator's first-person endorsement or their branding.

The persistent headline and changing speech subtitles are separate layers.
Place accurate phrase subtitles horizontally centered around the middle of
the visible picture rectangle, matching the references. Position relative to
the inner picture bounds, not the full portrait canvas or lower black margin.
Keep placement consistent across shots, reframing the speaker or making small
adjustments within the central picture area to protect the face. The fixed
headline remains above the picture. A graphic headline overlay is allowed;
it is not B-roll. Verify both layers across the entire editorial portion. Quiet speech
pauses and longer speaker holds can be meaningful; the 5-second B-roll cap
does not apply to this no-B-roll lane.

This lane has no default preference over the other three. Four lanes still
use the existing pool of at most three editors; an editor can take a different
lane on its next assignment.

## Headline weight and hierarchy

Use genuinely bold/heavy headline lettering in every lane with a headline.
The headline must read as the dominant title at phone size and have at least
the visible stroke weight of the speech captions. A large but thin headline
above thick captions fails this brief. Select an actual bold/heavy font face
and verify the rendered glyphs: a requested weight may fall back to a lighter
font, especially when text is generated as an image. Preserve existing caption
placement and timing; do not fix the hierarchy by making captions unreadable.

## Spoken-word caption highlighting

Across all four lanes, every interval containing speech uses word-timed
captions whose currently spoken word is visibly colored while the neighboring
words stay neutral. Normally show three or four words at a time; shorter groups
are valid at sentence boundaries, pauses, or when needed for legibility, but
never show more than four. The accent must move on the real word timings, not
on a sentence estimate, and must have enough contrast to be obvious at phone
size. Keep the accent palette coherent within a short instead of changing to
random colors on every word.

Use Valmera's real word-state renderer: a preset whose rendered output colors
the active spoken word, or legacy `dynamic:true` with `highlight_color`.
`max_words_per_caption` is at most 4. A static colored keyword, a caption card
that merely reveals words, or a configured color that is not visible in the
render does not satisfy this rule. Inspect multiple rendered states, including
the first caption, the densest phrase, a speaker or shot change, and the last
caption. Silent openings and Style 2's silent montage remain caption-free.

## Preserve Valmera branding and match its placement to the picture

Both the native corner watermark and the complete native "Edited by Valmera
AI" ending (including the platform's full wording, logo, animation, and
duration) are required. They are intentional user-owned marketing. Rejecting
third-party watermarked assets does not authorize stripping Valmera branding.
Do not crop, conceal, disable, replace, shorten, or bypass either brand element.
Do not substitute a preview or an unbranded local re-encode for the final.

Reserve the actual native ending duration within the 15–45-second final.
Shorten the editorial portion coherently when needed. The native ending is
allowed after every lane, including the otherwise silent montage and the
no-B-roll headline conversation. It is not filler, a forbidden external card,
or a reason to extend the topic headline over the native branding.

Use the existing admin page's **Export watermark → Watermark position**
choices. The user explicitly authorizes choosing the appropriate placement
through their authenticated admin session for this workflow; no separate
routine permission or product-change task is needed. This correction replaces
the earlier rigid top-right/upper-left acceptance rules. Match the picture
using the available native modes, rather than inventing coordinate controls.

Choices verified in `frontend-next/src/app/admin/page.js` on September 14:

| Admin choice | Behavior and use |
| --- | --- |
| Current corner (`frame`) | Native frame corner; suitable for full-frame portrait or square footage. |
| Lower corner (`lower`) | Same horizontal anchor, slightly lower; use when more top clearance improves the composition. |
| Scene top (`scene`) | Top of horizontal footage fitted inside a padded or blurred-padded 9:16 canvas; other layouts keep the native frame corner. Prefer for the brief's wide picture within portrait bars when applicable. |

Use the current setting if it already suits the composition. Otherwise the
coordinator chooses the appropriate mode once for compatible exports, checks
that the setting saved, and verifies the result in a final download. The
native corner need not exactly match an earlier illustrative side or pixel
coordinate to pass: it must be visible, legible, safely placed, and clear of
faces and text. Do not remove, cover, or duplicate the mark to reposition it.

Placement settings are shared. Only the coordinator changes them; editors
continue editing independently. Keep compatible final renders under the same
mode. Finish and download those renders before switching modes, since settings
apply to the next render and can invalidate cached downloads. Change placement
only; preserve the master/force toggles. Record the starting setting and restore
it after downloads if temporarily changed, unless the user asked to retain the
new setting or another actor has since changed it. This needs no per-clip pause.

The authenticated admin UI is the normal route; its existing
`/admin/video/settings` operation accepts `watermark_position` values `frame`,
`lower`, or `scene`. Do not bypass access controls or edit production code to
use these choices. If access is temporarily unavailable, continue editing and
use the current native placement when it passes visual QC. An unavailable
exact corner is not a batch failure. Record any remaining limitation once and
keep progressing on deliverable work instead of repeatedly reporting it.

The native end card is added to finals separately from EDL program time;
local configuration defaults to five seconds but may differ in the running
app. Reserve its actual duration and inspect its presence in the download.

## Word-card synchronization

Whenever a word/name card emphasizes something being said, synchronize it
with that exact spoken occurrence, whether the interviewer or guest says it.
The first readable frame should coincide with the word's audible onset,
rounded to the nearest output frame. Any entrance animation must make the
word readable at that cue, rather than beginning a slow reveal there. Use
word-level timing, not a sentence timestamp or a guessed visual beat.

Map source word times through the final cuts, inserts, and speed changes.
Recalculate after any timeline change and listen to the rendered audio while
checking the card's appearance; transcript timestamps alone do not prove sync.
Repeated words must be matched to the intended occurrence. Preserve continuous
speech under the card with an overlay or equivalent supported treatment;
do not insert a scene that pauses or shifts the word away from its card.
Choose the hold for readability and the lane's pacing after aligning onset.

This applies to speech-emphasis cards wherever that lane permits them. It
does not add cards to Style 4. Standalone labels in Style 2's silent montage
follow its visual sequence and must not bring back speech or dialogue captions.

## Current default composition and optional rounded cards

For the user's current brief, use the rectangular picture on a black 9:16
canvas described in `headline-conversation-references-v7.md` by default.
The persistent full-duration headline is mandatory for Style 4; the other
styles retain their authored headline/caption timing and audio structure.
In Style 2, full-frame montage means the entire active picture panel, with no
podcast visible behind it; it need not fill the outer black-canvas margins.

Rounded video cards remain an optional treatment when chosen for a specific
short. They are no longer the user's default for every short.

When requested, inset the video on an intentional background with visibly
rounded corners, modest margins, and optional subtle shadow. This is distinct
from the outer canvas ratio. Choose the ratio from the brief and inspected
references; do not interpret "widget card" as an instruction for 4:5.

The advertised `set_screen_frame` treatment affects the whole finished picture,
including captions and overlays. Start with a modest inset and corner radius,
then inspect actual pixels at phone size. Text placed near the top must remain
inside the inset picture's safe area unless a separately verified composition
can place it outside. Do not promise an independently placed outside header
using this effect alone. Verify the current schema before setting parameters.

`set_frame` selects source, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, or 4:5 in the inspected catalog;
it controls outer shape rather than rounded corners. Check the current tool
schema and final file, and review every speaker change and B-roll crop.

## Implementation and review traps

- For Style 2, trim the podcast keep list to the complete spoken hook and
  append actual montage scenes. Overlays do not remove underlying dialogue.
  Check the final timeline for leftover podcast footage after the inserts.
- `insert_media` can preserve an inserted video's own audio. Main-source
  `set_volume` does not mute that audio. Use a supported clip-mute route, or
  prepare a silent derivative and upload it before insertion. Verify silence
  over the complete intended interval in the rendered file.
- Insert positions and headline windows use output time; many source cuts and
  source audio operations use source time. Recompute cue times after inserts,
  trims, and speed changes instead of reusing stale timestamps.
- In the inspected implementation, `add_title_card` clamps to at least 0.6
  seconds; image inserts can hold from 0.2 seconds. Use a designed image for a
  supported shorter card, or deliberately lengthen the hold. Read the live
  response; do not report a requested duration that was silently clamped.
- Native title-card defaults can return to underlying footage. Their use
  does not establish the no-return montage structure; verify the actual order.
- In the inspected `add_text` contract, the text item mutes transcript
  captions for its whole duration. Do not add a full-duration headline that
  accidentally hides all spoken subtitles. Use a verified separate headline
  route, such as an uploaded transparent text PNG with `add_overlay`, which
  leaves captions enabled, or a live tool explicitly supporting coexistence.
  Re-enabling captions alone does not undo a text item's suppression.
- Clean source asset audio under a continuing conversation must not compete
  with dialogue. No added music or SFX when the user is adding music later.
- A 15–45-second brief applies to the entire final, including opener, montage,
  cards, and any export-added tail. Do not exceed the user's limit to preserve
  a generic story template or a tool default.
- For Style 2, the newer lane-specific limit supersedes that generic range:
  no more than 25 seconds of editorial program, no more than 15 seconds of
  montage, followed by the complete separate native Valmera ending.
- Preserve speaker meaning, clear identity, quality assets, consistent type,
  and intentional content at every moment. Do not use unmotivated punch zooms
  to simulate pace. Evaluate all lane-specific blockers before scoring polish.
